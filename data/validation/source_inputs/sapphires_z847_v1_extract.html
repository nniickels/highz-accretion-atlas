This study is based on the JWST Cycle‑3 Treasury Survey, Slitless Areal Pure‑Parallel HIgh‑Redshift Emission Survey (SAPPHIRES; GO‑6434; PI: E. Egami). SAPPHIRES performs imaging and WFSS using 711​hours711\,{\rm hours} of pure‑parallel NIRCam observations. Within the SAPPHIRES data, we focus on the SAPPHIRES Early Data Release (EDR), which was obtained in a parallel field of the MACS0416 lensing cluster. Observations, photometry, and spectroscopy for the SAPPHIRES‑EDR are presented in Sun et al. [86]; interested readers are referred to the EDR paper for details.

In summary, with a total dual-channel exposure time of 47.2​hours47.2\,{\rm hours}, the SAPPHIRES‑EDR obtained NIRCam imaging in 13 medium‑ and broad‑band filters, as well as F356W and F444W NIRCam WFSS data in both the orthogonal Grism‑C and Grism‑R dispersion directions. Typically, the imaging observations reached 5​σ5\,\sigma sensitivities of ∼29​AB​mag\sim 29\,{\rm AB\ mag} for broad-band filters. The grism observations achieved a typical 5​σ5\,\sigma emission‑line sensitivity of 9×10−19​erg​s−1​cm−29\times 10^{-19}\,{\rm erg\,s^{-1}\,cm^{-2}} at 4.7​μ​m4.7\,{\rm\mu m} (i.e., the expected wavelength of [O iii] 5008​Å5008\,{\rm\AA} at z∼8.5z\sim 8.5) for the NIRCam module A and is ∼20%\sim 20\,\% less sensitive for module B. The emission line sensitivity typically corresponds to a star formation rate (SFR) sensitivity of ∼1​M⊙​yr−1\sim 1\,{\rm M_{\odot}\,yr^{-1}} based on previous [O iii] 5008​Å5008\,{\rm\AA} observations [94, 70].

Although the observed field is next to the lensing cluster MACS0416, the field is substantially distant (≳4′\gtrsim 4^{\prime}) from the lensing cluster. Based on the cluster mass model of Richard et al. [79], we conclude that the gravitational magnification effects from the cluster are negligible (μ≲1.1\mu\lesssim 1.1).

III.2 Photometric Galaxy Searches

In addition to the spectroscopic sample, we searched for candidate member galaxies using their photometric redshifts and tentative emission lines detected in their spectra. The photometric redshift-based searches aim to identify galaxies that, despite being faint in emission lines, show features placing them at z∼8z\sim 8 – 99, such as a Lyα\alpha break between the F090W and F150W images and flux excess in the F444W images resulting from weak contamination by emission lines. We expect that these line-faint galaxies include temporarily quenched galaxies [64, 102, e.g.,], dust-obscured galaxies [34, 5, e.g.,], and low-mass galaxies that are below our robust line detection limits.

Similar to Helton et al. [41], we selected photometric candidates using their accurate photometric redshifts according to the following four criteria: (i) both zmapz_{\rm map} (the maximum likelihood redshift) and zmedz_{\rm med} (the median redshift obtained using eazy) lie within 8.47±0.28.47\pm 0.2. (ii) The F444W magnitude is brighter than 29.1​mag29.1\,{\rm mag}, which corresponds to the median 5​σ5\,\sigma limiting magnitude of the F444W image. (iii) The 16​th16\,{\rm th} – 84​th84\,{\rm th} percentile interval of photometric redshift is smaller than 20%20\% of 1+zmed1+z_{\rm med}. (iv) The 5​th5\,{\rm th} – 95​th95\,{\rm th} percentile interval is smaller than 40%40\% of 1+zmed1+z_{\rm med}.

Using these criteria, we selected 1717 galaxies. Among these, 44 galaxies are already spectroscopically confirmed as members in this work, while 55 additional galaxies have spectroscopic redshifts ranging zspec=6.29z_{\rm spec}=6.29 to 8.158.15, indicating that they are not members [86]. Among the remaining photometric sample, one galaxy (ID-16702) shows a weak ∼3​σ\sim 3\,\sigma signal at the expected wavelength of [O iii] 5008​Å5008\,{\rm\AA} from z=8.482z=8.482 (Figure A1). Indeed, ID-16702 has a photometric redshift consistent with zmap=8.48z_{\rm map}=8.48, suggesting it is highly likely to be a member of MACS0416-OD-z8p5. Finally, we identified 77 photometric candidates that lack any credible emission line detections in their spectra (see Table B1 in the Appendix).

In our following analysis, we treat only ID-16702 as a tentative spectroscopic candidate and consider it as a member of MACS0416-OD-z8p5. However, the other 77 photometric candidates are regarded solely as potential candidates and are not formally counted as members of the over-density when analyzing its properties. Although our photometric redshifts are relatively accurate, spectroscopic redshifts remain essential for confirmation, especially since 55 galaxies have been spectroscopically confirmed to lie at different redshifts.

Compared to the final spectroscopic sample, the photometric candidates generally have higher dust attenuation and older ages (see Table B1). These features suggest that we might be missing such highly dust-attenuated and/or older stellar population dominated galaxies, that are faint in emission lines. Nevertheless, future spectroscopic confirmations are essential to investigate this further.

ID-25528

Among the photometric candidates, ID-25528 (RA=63.9968695{\rm RA}=63.9968695, Dec=−24.1184662{\rm Dec}=-24.1184662) stands out for its brightness (mF200W=25.7​mag{m}_{\rm F200W}=25.7\,{\rm mag}) and its secure photometric redshift (zph=8.60±0.1z_{\rm ph}=8.60\pm 0.1). The accuracy of the photometric redshift is supported by the clear Lyman-α\alpha break in the F115W image and the enhanced F444W flux, which is fully consistent with the presence of strong Hβ\beta and [O iii] λ​λ​4959, 5008​Å\lambda\lambda 4959,\,5008\,{\rm\AA} lines (Figure 2). If this redshift is confirmed, ID-25528 would rank among the most luminous galaxies at z>8z>8 (MUV=−21.8​magM_{\rm UV}=-21.8\,{\rm mag}) with JWST’s spectroscopic confirmation [80, 27, e.g.,]. Unfortunately, our grism observations capture ID-25528 only at the edge of the field of view and only cover a limited wavelength range (3.13.1 – 4.3​μ​m4.3\,{\rm\mu m}). Thus, no clear emission lines are detected in this wavelength range, as expected from its estimated redshift. Additionally, a nearby bright foreground galaxy affects its continuum subtraction and elevates the noise in the spectra, preventing detection of other fainter emission lines, such as Hγ\gamma and [Oii]λ​λ,3727,3730\,\lambda\lambda,3727,3730 at the shorter wavelength.

Therefore, there is no conclusive evidence that ID-25528 is part of MACS0416-OD-z8p5. However, recent observations suggest that massive star-forming galaxies tend to reside in galaxy over-densities [40, 65, e.g.,]. The apparently large size and tidal tail-like feature of ID-25528 suggest possible recent merger events induced by a dense surrounding environment, such as the paired galaxies ID-17832/ID-17882 and ID1967/ID1988 in the spectroscopic sample. This feature, thus, makes ID-25528 more likely to be part of MACS0416-OD-z8p5. Follow-up spectroscopy of ID-25528 will be crucial for understanding massive galaxy formation within large-scale galaxy over-densities.

III.3 Emission Line Velocity and Flux Measurements

The [O iii] 5008​Å5008\,{\rm\AA} and Hβ\beta line fluxes are measured using extracted one-dimensional (1D) spectra. During the fitting process, we use a single Gaussian for each line, convolved with the line spread function (LSF) modeled for the NIRCam R and C grisms [87]. These fitting procedures are performed separately for spectra obtained using the Grism R and C. The central wavelengths, line widths, and fluxes are left free parameters for the [O iii] 5008​Å5008\,{\rm\AA} line. For [O iii] 4959​Å4959\,{\rm\AA} and Hβ\beta, the line widths are fixed to those of [O iii] 5008​Å5008\,{\rm\AA}, except for ID-8760, which appears to have larger Hβ\beta line width (see §V.2.1 below). When emission lines are not significantly detected (<3​σ<3\,\sigma), we calculate 3​σ3\,\sigma flux upper limits by quadratically summing the flux uncertainties of channels over 2×2\times the [O iii] 5008​Å5008\,{\rm\AA} line FWHM of each galaxy. When [O iii] 5008​Å5008\,{\rm\AA} line widths are unavailable due to contamination of nearby sources, we assume 150​km/s150\,{\rm km/s}. IV.2 Identifying Active Galactic Nuclei

JWST has identified a new population of galaxies that are candidates of faint active galactic nuclei (AGNs) [39, 52]. These galaxies are often named as “Little-Red-Dots” [68, LRD;] based on their apparent colors, morphology, and their broad Balmer emission lines. Despite extensive efforts so far, it is not fully elucidated how and where these LRDs emerge. Furthermore, LRD’s physical properties are not yet fully understood. Theoretical studies suggest an enhanced growth of central black holes of galaxies in dense regions. Recent studies identified several LRDs in a galaxy proto-cluter at high redshift [21, 56], although it remains unclear or could be unlikely that LRDs preferentially reside in over-dense environments. We examined if enhancement of the AGN population in over-density environments is already the case for this z∼8.5z\sim 8.5 galaxy over-density by searching for AGN candidates in MACS0416-OD-z8p5.

We used the photometric LRD criterion developed in Greene et al. [35] and applied the color and morphology classification for the spectroscopically confirmed samples and photometric candidates. Among the photometric candidates, we do not find any galaxies satisfying the criteria. Among the spectroscopic samples, ID-8760 is found to be fully consistent with the color-color criteria of LRD within ∼1​σ\sim 1\,\sigma (Figure 3). ID-8760 has fF444W​(0​.′′​4)f_{\rm F444W}(0\farcs 4)/fF444W​(0​.′′​2)=1.58±0.04f_{\rm F444W}(0\farcs 2)=1.58\pm 0.04, where fF444W​(0​.′′​4)f_{\rm F444W}(0\farcs 4) and fF444W​(0​.′′​2)f_{\rm F444W}(0\farcs 2) represents aperture flux densities measured using aperture diameters of 0​.′′​40\farcs 4 and 0​.′′​20\farcs 2, respectively. Recently, Greene et al. [35] used fF444W​(0​.′′​4)f_{\rm F444W}(0\farcs 4)/fF444W​(0​.′′​2)<1.5f_{\rm F444W}(0\farcs 2)<1.5 as a compactness criterion for LRD, which is slightly lower than that for ID-8760 [see also 57, for a compact criterion of 1.7]. However, we identified that there is a foreground galaxy next to the ID-8760, which contributes to elevating the fF444W​(0​.′′​4)f_{\rm F444W}(0\farcs 4). Thus, the current measurement of fF444W​(0​.′′​4)f_{\rm F444W}(0\farcs 4)/fF444W​(0​.′′​2)=1.58±0.04f_{\rm F444W}(0\farcs 2)=1.58\pm 0.04 is consistent with the compactness criterion of LRDs. These morphology and color criteria show that ID-8760 is indeed a good candidate to be an LRD and/or AGN.

In the spectrum (Figure 3), the Hβ\beta line of ID-8760 has a large full-width-at-half-maximum of 1010±190​km/s1010\pm 190\,{\rm km/s} while the H​βH\beta line is detected with a relatively low SNR (peak SNR of 2.5​σ2.5\,\sigma). Combining the color-color criteria, the compactness, and the large width exceeding 1000​km/s1000\,{\rm km/s}, we conclude that it is indeed a spectroscopically confirmed LRD/AGN at z=8.47z=8.47. Thus, ID-8740 is among the highest redshift LRDs so far confirmed using JWST spectroscopy [53, 59].

V.2 Structure of MACS0416-OD-z8p5

V.2.1 LRD in the MACS0416-OD-z8p5

In MACS0416-OD-z8p5, we identified an LRD and/or AGN candidate (ID-8760). ID-8760 is located in the south west part of the current footprint (Figure 1) where the surface density of galaxies is lower compared with the north east side of the over-density.

Champagne et al. [20] similarly find AGNs are located in outer edge regions of galaxy proto-clusters. The authors discussed that the outer parts of proto-clusters are the preferred location for AGNs to emerge due to the large amount of materials funneling down from large structures such as in the cosmic web. Morishita et al. [71] reported the environmental conditions for the emergence of AGN and find that an AGN/LRD was found to be isolated from other galaxies, possibly avoiding the most dense regions. The authors discussed that the negative feedback would also influence the growth of surrounding galaxies. Although interpretations based on the single detection of an LRD in the over-density’s isolated area would be uncertain, our discovery of ID-8760 may be consistent with the scenarios where the emergence of LRD or AGN may have a dependence on environment and through feedback, affect surrounding galaxies.

V.2.2 Known Galaxy Over-Density Candidate at z=8.31z=8.31 to z=8.49z=8.49

In the gravitational lensing field behind the MACS0416 cluster, multiple massive galaxies are known to exist and they potentially form a galaxy over-density at z≳8z\gtrsim 8. One of the most representative galaxies is MACS0416-Y1 [4, 89, 90]. Recently, two galaxies were spectroscopically found to exist at a similar redshift (MACS0416-JD at z=8.34z=8.34 and f090d_018 at z=8.49z=8.49) [62, 65]. Although these galaxies are located away from MACS0416-OD-z8p5 (with a separation of 2.8​arcmin2.8\,{\rm arcmin} or ∼0.8​pMpc\sim 0.8\,{\rm pMpc}), the existence of nearby massive galaxies may suggest there exists a large scale structure further connecting these separate fields. In particular, we find that when we find galaxy over-densities at z=8z=8 – 99 in the UniverseMachine (see §V.1.2), typically the galaxies in over-densities with SFR>2​M⊙​yr−1{\rm SFR>2\,{\rm M_{\odot}\,yr^{-1}}} extend over ∼2\sim 2 – 4​arcmin4\,{\rm arcmin} (∼0.5\sim 0.5–1.1​pMpc1.1\,{\rm pMpc}), suggesting that such an extended galaxy over-density may exist.

However detailed studies of the connection between MACS0416-OD-z8p5 and MACS0416-Y1 need further data between the SAPPHIRES-EDR field and the MACS0416 cluster field areas to confirm the large-scale structure. To further expand the study comparing MACS0416-OD-z8p5 with theoretical expectations (§V.1.2), environmental effects of AGN (§V.2.1), and large scale structure (§V.2.2), further WFSS observations of galaxies at large physical separations are essential as they are outside of the current SAPPHIRES observations.

VI Conclusions

In this study, we reported the discovery and analyses of a galaxy proto-cluster candidate, MACS0416-OD-z8p5, at z∼8.47z\sim 8.47. The proto-cluster candidate was identified using JWST NIRCam’s WFSS obtained as part of the cycle-3 treasury program SAPPHIRES. From the deep NIRCam imaging and WFSS observations covering a ∼16.5​arcmin2\sim 16.5\,{\rm arcmin^{2}} field around MACS0416 cluster, we found the following results:

∙\bullet From a sample of 99 galaxies with secure spectroscopic redshifts and one galaxy with a tentative spectroscopic redshift, we robustly identify a galaxy overdensity at z∼8.47z\sim 8.47, named MACS0416-OD-z8p5. The high concentration of spectroscopically confirmed galaxies in a small volume allows us to identify the highest redshift spectroscopically confirmed galaxy over-density currently known, with a density enhancement factor of δ∼6\delta\sim 6 – 88. Four of the member galaxies are part of two close pair systems, suggesting on-going interactions or major mergers. The relatively high merger rate, the two interacting system out of eight systems, possibly explain the accelerated galaxy evolution within dense protocluster environments.

∙\bullet While the total number of confirmed sources depends on the interpretation of the close-paired galaxies, the density enhancement (δ∼6\delta\sim 6–88) exceeds predicted conditions for a galaxy proto-cluster [22]. Therefore, we conclude that MACS0416-OD-z8p5 is a candidate system that will become a galaxy cluster in the present day Universe.

∙\bullet In MACS0416-OD-z8p5, we confirm that one of its members (ID-8760) is a candidate low mass AGN or “Little-Red-Dot” (LRD) at z=8.47z=8.47. ID-8760 meets the color-color selection criteria of LRD from Greene et al. [35], although the compactness criterion is marginally satisfied due to the minor contamination from a nearby foreground galaxy. The Hβ\beta emission line shows a large line width of FWHM=1010±190​km/s{\rm FWHM}=1010\pm 190\,{\rm km/s}, albeit with a relatively low signal-to-noise ratio (peak SNR of 2.5​σ2.5\,\sigma). Based on these criteria, ID-8760 is confirmed to be among the highest-redshift LRDs. The relatively isolated environment of this LRD within the over-density is in-line with previously identifications of LRDs in proto-clusters, suggesting that environmental effects may influence LRD formation and/or the LRD’s negative feedback on formation of surrounding environments.

∙\bullet We estimate the total dark matter halo mass of log⁡(Mhalo/M⊙)=11.4−0.1+0.1{\rm log}(M_{\rm halo}/{\rm M_{\odot}})=11.4^{+0.1}_{-0.1} using stellar-to-halo-mass ratio from Behroozi et al. [6], and log⁡(Mhalo/M⊙)=11.7{\rm log}(M_{\rm halo}/{\rm M_{\odot}})=11.7 from UV luminosity to halo mass relation from Mason et al. [67]. These estimates suggest the MACS0416-OD-z8p5 is hosted within one of the most massive dark matter halos at z>8z>8.

∙\bullet By comparing the number of galaxies with those simulated [6], we find that the probability of finding such a massive halo in the limited survey area of 16.5​arcmin216.5\,{\rm arcmin^{2}} is low (<4<4 – 7%7\,\%). If the discrepancy between observations and simulations is confirmed, it may suggest that our current understanding about galaxy evolution in dense galaxy environments needs some revision, such as higher baryon conversion efficiency or larger amounts of gas accretion.

∙\bullet MACS0416-OD-z8p5 is located ∼5​pMpc\sim 5\,{\rm pMpc} away from the previously identified massive galaxies and potential galaxy over-density at z=8.31z=8.31 – 8.498.49, discovered behind the lensing cluster (i.e., MACS0416-Y1, JD, f090d_018; e.g., Tamura et al. 89, Li et al. 62, Ma et al. 65). These findings in a small field suggest the existence of a large scale structure connecting these regions, making this region important to investigate early galaxy formation in dense environments.

The identification of MACS0416-OD-z8p5 demonstrates the power of combining deep multi-wavelength imaging and WFSS, providing a complete sample of line emitters and to search for galaxy proto-clusters at very high redshift. Future observations covering larger areas in this field will help characterize the spatial extent and richness of this over-density. Similarly, future NIRSpec observations of the rest-optical emission lines and continuum will constrain the nebular and stellar population properties for the member galaxies alongside representative field galaxies. Furthermore, the completion of the SAPPHIRES observations will enable an evaluation of accurate number densities of galaxy over-densities at high redshifts, an essential study to constrain phenomena that are responsible for the formation of massive galaxy over-density hosted by massive dark matter halos.
