\author{Nicole Jiang}
\date{Draft: 11 September 2026}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Aims.} Massive black holes in the early Universe must grow within limited
cosmic time. We identify which JWST black-hole candidates most strongly constrain
early growth models and which assumptions control these constraints.
\textbf{Methods.} We compile 350 literature measurement records from 32 source
families at $z\geq4$. We analyse 220 objects with broad hydrogen-line mass
estimates and include 14 additional objects in a broader comparison. We propagate
reported mass uncertainties and test mass-scale shifts and alternative growth
assumptions.
\textbf{Results.} For $100\,M_\odot$ seeds formed at $z=30$, radiative efficiency
$\epsilon=0.1$, and no merger boost, 8 of the 220 objects require a lifetime-average
Eddington ratio $\overline{f}_{\mathrm{Edd,req}}>1$. Of these, 5 exceed this threshold
in at least 95\% of trials sampling their quoted mass errors under fixed growth
assumptions. For UNCOVER-20466, COSMOS3D-13852, and RUBIES-EGS-55604, at least
95\% of trials still require an average Eddington ratio above 1 after
reducing each mass by $0.5$ dex (a factor of approximately 3.2).
Uncertainties in the mass estimators also affect these constraints.
Delaying seeding to $z=20$ makes GN-z11 the most demanding object.
For A2744-QSO1, an independent dynamical mass estimate raises the required
average Eddington ratio to approximately 1.
At $\epsilon=0.05719$, the ideal thin-disc efficiency for a
non-spinning black hole, all 234 central mass estimates are reachable from
$100\,M_\odot$ seeds formed at $z=30$ without lifetime-average accretion above
the Eddington limit.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Key words.} black hole physics -- accretion, accretion disks --
galaxies: active -- galaxies: high-redshift -- quasars: supermassive black holes
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Massive black holes observed in the early Universe must have assembled within
the available cosmic time. The strength of this constraint depends on the observed
black-hole mass and redshift and on assumptions about seed formation,
radiative efficiency, and the duration of active accretion.

We compile a literature-based catalogue of JWST-identified high-redshift
accreting black holes and candidates at $z\geq4$, extending from the first
billion years to a cosmic age of approximately 1.54 Gyr at its lower-redshift
boundary. We retain published measurements, object identity links and
mass-estimation sources so that measurements from different studies can be compared.

Analytic seed-growth comparisons already establish the trade-off between seed
mass and sustained accretion. For example, \citet{dayal2024} considered
light and heavy seeds at $z_{\rm seed}=25$ and explored a primordial-seeding
interpretation. We apply the same growth equations to a larger catalogue to
calculate whether each black hole could reach its observed mass while accreting,
on average, at or below the Eddington limit. We test how the required rates
change with seed and growth assumptions, masses drawn from published uncertainty
intervals, uniform mass shifts, and alternative published measurements.
For $100\,M_\odot$ seeds formed at $z=30$, radiative efficiency $\epsilon=0.1$
and no mass added by mergers, 8 objects in our Balmer-line sample require an
average Eddington ratio above 1. We list these objects and assess how the
limitations reported for their mass estimates affect the growth constraints.

For A2744-QSO1, we compare the catalogue's
virial mass with an independent dynamical estimate.

The contributing studies include the JADES broad-line census
\citep{juodzbalis2026}, which measures current accretion and host scaling relations,
and the narrow-line census \citep{scholtz2025}, which identifies candidates for
obscured activity. The studies targeted different kinds of objects and reached
different detection limits. Their combined probability of selecting and detecting
a black hole is unknown. The source-family inventory is given in
Appendix~\ref{app:sources}.

\subsection{Theoretical framework}
\label{sec:theory}

We use the luminosity-based Eddington ratio $\fedd=L_{\rm bol}/L_{\rm Edd}$,
where $L_{\rm bol}$ is the total luminosity of the accretion flow integrated
over all wavelengths and $L_{\rm Edd}$ is the luminosity at which outward
radiation force balances gravity for electron scattering in ionized hydrogen.
The fixed radiative efficiency $\epsilon$ is the fraction of inflowing
rest-mass energy emitted as radiation; the fraction $1-\epsilon$ is retained
by the black hole in this model. We define the available growth interval
$\Delta t$ and the lifetime-average Eddington ratio $\overline{f}_{\mathrm{Edd}}$ as
\begin{equation}
\Delta t=t(z_{\rm obs})-t(z_{\rm seed})>0,\qquad
\overline{f}_{\mathrm{Edd}}=\frac{1}{\Delta t}
\int_{t(z_{\rm seed})}^{t(z_{\rm obs})}\fedd(t)\,dt.
\label{eq:average}
\end{equation}
Here $t(z)$ is the age of the Universe at redshift $z$, $z_{\rm seed}$ is
the redshift at which the seed forms and growth begins, and $z_{\rm obs}$ is
the observed redshift. The integration variable $t$ is cosmic time, and
$\fedd(t)$ is the instantaneous Eddington ratio; its average includes both
active and quiet phases throughout $\Delta t$.
We use the exponential growth relation adopted by \citet{dayal2024},
expressed in terms of the time-averaged Eddington ratio, and introduce
a multiplier $B_{\rm merge}$ to approximate additional mass from mergers:
\begin{equation}
\mbh(t_{\rm obs})=B_{\rm merge}\mseed
\exp\!\left[\frac{1-\epsilon}{\epsilon}
\frac{\overline{f}_{\mathrm{Edd}}\,\Delta t}{t_{\rm Edd}}\right],
\label{eq:growth}
\end{equation}
Here $\mbh(t_{\rm obs})$ is the black-hole mass at the observation time
$t_{\rm obs}=t(z_{\rm obs})$, and $\mseed$ is the initial seed mass.
The Eddington timescale, $t_{\rm Edd}=\mbh c^2/L_{\rm Edd}\simeq0.45$ Gyr,
is the time needed to radiate an energy equal to the black hole's rest-mass
energy at its Eddington luminosity, with $c$ denoting the speed of light.
Because the Eddington luminosity is proportional to black-hole mass,
$t_{\rm Edd}\simeq0.45$ Gyr is the same for all black-hole masses under
this assumption. The mass-growth e-folding time also depends on efficiency and
accretion rate: for constant $\fedd>0$, it is
$[\epsilon/(1-\epsilon)]t_{\rm Edd}/\fedd$, or 0.05 Gyr for
$\epsilon=0.1$ and $\fedd=1$.
The dimensionless merger multiplier is
$B_{\rm merge}=1$ for the reference calculation and 2 for a factor-of-two
mass boost. We represent mergers by multiplying the seed mass by a constant
factor before calculating accretion. This approximation omits the timing and
sequence of mergers, their mass ratios, and mass lost through gravitational radiation.
The required lifetime-average ratio $\overline{f}_{\mathrm{Edd,req}}$ is the
average needed to reach the observed mass $\mbh$ from the assumed seed
within $\Delta t$:
\begin{equation}
\overline{f}_{\mathrm{Edd,req}}
=\max\!\left[0,\frac{\epsilon}{1-\epsilon}
\frac{t_{\rm Edd}}{\Delta t}
\ln\!\left(\frac{\mbh}{B_{\rm merge}\mseed}\right)\right].
\label{eq:required-rate}
\end{equation}
If $B_{\rm merge}M_{\rm seed}$ exceeds the observed black-hole mass, the
logarithm is negative and we set the required average Eddington ratio to zero.
Even with zero accretion, this seed and merger contribution predict a mass
larger than observed. Reproducing the observed mass would require a smaller
seed, a smaller merger contribution, or a model that includes mass loss.
This requirement averages accretion over the full growth interval. Inferring
a duty cycle also requires assumptions about the rates during active and quiet phases.

We calculate cosmic ages using the present-day Hubble expansion rate
$H_0=67.3\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}$ and matter density parameter
$\Omega_m=0.315$, matching these 2 values in \citet{dayal2024},
who cite Planck. We impose spatial flatness and set
$\Omega_\Lambda=1-\Omega_m=0.685$, where $\Omega_\Lambda$ is the density
parameter of the cosmological constant; both density parameters are relative
to the present-day critical density. The age calculation includes matter
and a cosmological constant and neglects radiation. Appendix~\ref{app:derivations}
gives the supporting relations, implementation, and inverse seed-mass diagnostic.
Equations~\ref{eq:growth}--\ref{eq:required-rate} assume constant radiative efficiency.
The ideal thin-disc efficiencies used in Figure~\ref{fig:allgrowth} are
0.03775, 0.05719, and 0.42265 for dimensionless spins $a=-1,0,+1$,
respectively \citep{bardeen1972}. Here $a$ is the black-hole spin parameter:
$a=-1$ and $+1$ denote maximal spins counter-aligned and aligned with the
disc's angular momentum, respectively, and $a=0$ denotes a non-spinning
black hole. Each track holds efficiency constant;
spin evolution is unspecified.

\section{Catalogue construction}

We compile 350 literature measurements from 32 source families at $z\geq4$.
Each adopted mass is linked to its source, method, and reported uncertainty.
We adopt one measurement per object and retain alternatives for sensitivity tests.
Choices follow the documented source assessments. For example, CEERS-2782 uses
the higher-signal-to-noise RUBIES-EGS-50052 spectrum preferred by the source study,
while NX10835 uses the newly available NEXUS mass estimate. The measurement--object
link table records the adopted measurement, publication version, and reason for each choice.

Growth analysis requires a numerical mass and redshift, an identified mass
method, and a resolved lensing treatment. Upper limits, indirect model ranges,
and masses inferred by assuming an Eddington ratio are excluded from numerical
inference. We also exclude 6 catalogue entries in 3 groups where it
remains unclear whether entries from different studies refer to the same
physical object. Three of these entries have eligible masses. This leaves
220 primary objects and an inclusive exploratory sample of 234 objects.

For the main comparison, we use masses estimated from broad H$\alpha$ or
H$\beta$ emission lines in objects with secure or probable evidence for an
accreting black hole, using the evidence classifications recorded from the
source papers. Seven masses are explicitly flagged as conditional on a
broad-line-region interpretation: 6 candidates with intermediate-width
H$\alpha$ emission and possible outflow contamination \citep{ren2025}, and
RUBIES-EGS-49140 \citep{taylor2025},
for which an alternative non-AGN interpretation is recorded. These 7
objects enter the expanded sample only. Four further JADES objects---GS-20057765,
GS-20030333, GS-164055, and GN-4685---enter the exploratory sample only because
their individual broad-H$\beta$ detections are tentative, although broad emission
is supported by the stacked spectrum \citep{juodzbalis2026}.
GLIMPSE-38548 and GLIMPSE-7404 also enter the exploratory sample only;
the catalogue classifies them as tentative broad-H$\alpha$ AGN \citep{fei2026}.
After the identity exclusions,
the main comparison contains 220 objects, labelled primary in the tables
and figures. Other limitations of a virial mass, including possible scattering,
are recorded separately. COSMOS3D-13852 remains in the main sample on the basis
of its published broad-line AGN identification; we test the reported scattering
bias in Section~\ref{sec:credibility}.
These single-epoch virial estimators combine the line width, which estimates
gas velocity, with a luminosity-based estimate of the broad-line region's radius.
Their calibration is based on reverberation mapping of nearby AGN \citep{shen2013}.
Using the same line family and mass-estimation framework reduces differences
between methods in the comparison. Applying these calibrations at high redshift
still requires assessing gas dynamics, geometry, obscuration, and lensing for
each object.
We also analyse an expanded sample of 234 objects, labelled exploratory in
the tables and figures. It includes the 220 primary objects, 13 additional
candidates, and GN-z11. GN-z11's mass is inferred from rest-frame ultraviolet
emission lines using local virial relations \citep{maiolino2024}. We include
it in the expanded comparison because its lines and calibration differ from
those of the Balmer-line sample. The sample division reflects the evidence
for accretion and the methods being compared; the reliability of each mass
estimate requires an individual assessment.
When a paper gives a mass without an uncertainty, we can calculate the average
Eddington ratio needed to reach that mass. We cannot calculate the probability
that this ratio exceeds 1 from the published information, because no mass
uncertainty distribution is available. We therefore include these objects in
calculations at their published masses and omit them from probability calculations.
The mass estimates retained in each sample still use different calibrations,
and the contributing studies have different target-selection criteria and
detection limits.
Appendix~\ref{app:sources} gives the source inventory,
identity decisions, and sample accounting. Table~\ref{tab:scale} summarizes
the catalogue and analysis sample sizes.

$\epsilon=0.1$, and $B_{\rm merge}=1$ as the reference history.
We adopt $z_{\rm seed}=30$ as an early starting point that allows more time
for light-seed growth, and test how later onset changes the required accretion rates.
We rank objects by this required average Eddington ratio.

\subsection{Uncertainty and sensitivity}
\label{sec:uncertainty}

We use Monte Carlo sampling to propagate reported asymmetric log-mass errors,
with 10,000 draws per object. Each draw has equal probability of falling above
or below the central mass, with its distance drawn from a half-normal
distribution scaled by the corresponding upper or lower error. Redshifts are
held fixed. For each sampled mass, we calculate the average Eddington ratio
needed to grow from the assumed seed to that mass. The fraction of the 10,000
Monte Carlo draws with a required ratio above 1 estimates the probability
that growth requires an average rate above the Eddington limit, given the
adopted mass-error distribution and growth assumptions.
Probability calculations use objects with reported mass uncertainties (Table~\ref{tab:scale}).

We test mass-scale sensitivity by shifting all adopted log masses and their
error draws by offsets of $-0.5$, $-0.3$, $0$, $+0.3$, and $+0.5$ dex, keeping the sample and growth
assumptions fixed. These fixed offsets are stress tests at the uncertainty scale of single-epoch
mass estimates \citep{shen2013}.
For each offset, we report the fraction of shifted Monte Carlo draws that
require an average Eddington ratio above 1. Each probability assumes that
particular mass shift and the stated seed mass, starting redshift, radiative
efficiency, and merger contribution. Sampling and conversion
details are given in Appendix~\ref{app:implementation}.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Required accretion under the reference assumptions}

For a $10^2\,M_\odot$ seed under the reference history, the median required
lifetime-average Eddington ratio across the 220 primary objects is
$\overline{f}_{\mathrm{Edd,req}}=0.573$ (interquartile range 0.485--0.673),
with 8 point requirements above unity and none above 2. Across the
inclusive 234-object sample, the corresponding median is 0.574
(interquartile range 0.488--0.679), with 14 above unity. These sample
statistics use each object's point requirement.
UNCOVER-20466 is most demanding: its point requirement is 1.452, while the
median required lifetime-average Eddington ratio over its mass-error draws
is 1.453 (16th--84th percentiles 1.354--1.549).
Table~\ref{tab:top} lists the 8 primary objects above the threshold; their ordering by
point requirement differs from ordering by quoted-error probability.

The shorter growth time at $z=10.603$ places GN-z11 second in the required-rate
ranking despite its smaller mass. Its UV virial estimator places it in
the exploratory sample.

\subsection{External dynamical-mass check: A2744-QSO1}

These constraints do not uniquely identify a seed-formation history. With
radiative efficiency $\epsilon=0.05719$ for a non-spinning black hole,
$100\,M_\odot$ seeds formed at $z=30$, and no merger contribution, all adopted
central masses can be reached without a lifetime-average Eddington ratio above 1.

\subsection{Growth constraints for individual objects}

UNCOVER-20466 and GS-20057765 require average Eddington ratios above 1
for $10^3M_\odot$ seeds, but below 1 for $10^5M_\odot$ seeds, with the other
reference assumptions unchanged. At radiative efficiency $\epsilon=0.05719$,
$100\,M_\odot$ seeds formed at $z=30$ without merger growth require average
Eddington ratios of 0.793 and 0.740, respectively. If accretion occurs at the
Eddington limit during active phases and ceases between them, these values
require activity for about 79\% and 74\% of the available time.
The constraints for GS-20057765 depend on its tentative broad-H$\beta$
component tracing a broad-line region.

GN-z11 requires an average Eddington ratio of 1.437 under the reference
assumptions. Delaying seeding to $z=20$ raises this to 1.880, above
UNCOVER-20466's 1.733, reversing their order. A mass estimate based on the
Balmer lines would test whether this result depends on GN-z11's UV mass
estimator. Appendix~\ref{app:seed-timing} shows how seed mass and starting
redshift affect the required average Eddington ratios for both objects.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

The contributing studies use different selection criteria and detection
limits. Inferring population demographics would require a combined completeness
model. Virial calibration and geometry errors are incompletely reported.
The mass shifts test how the results respond to systematic changes in mass.

Two duplicate pairs have been reconciled using the source papers; the identities
of 3 groups remain unresolved
(Appendix~\ref{app:identities}). Excluding all 6 affected records preserves
the threshold results. Reinstating these records requires checking their spectra, masses, images, or
apertures against the source papers. Counts of unique objects and hosts remain
provisional.

Equation~\ref{eq:growth} describes growth through an average accretion rate,
a fixed merger multiplier, and constant radiative efficiency. Feedback and
individual accretion histories remain unspecified. The probabilities hold
these assumptions fixed. Averaging over them would require justified
parameter distributions and correlations; identifying seed origins would
also require formation models and population information.

\section{Conclusions}

For the adopted masses and growth assumptions, we find:
\begin{itemize}
\item For $100\,M_\odot$ seeds formed at $z=30$, $\epsilon=0.1$, and no
merger contribution, 8 of the 220 objects in the main sample require an
average Eddington ratio above 1. Five do so in at least 95\% of Monte Carlo
draws from their quoted mass-error distributions.
\item For UNCOVER-20466, COSMOS3D-13852, and RUBIES-EGS-55604, at least
95\% of draws still require an average Eddington ratio above 1 after
reducing the masses by 0.5 dex. Their mass estimates require tests of lensing,
scattering, and calibration, respectively.
\item The assumed start of growth changes which objects require average
accretion above the Eddington limit and which requires the highest rate.
At constant efficiency $\epsilon=0.05719$, every central mass in the
234-object expanded sample is reachable from a $100\,M_\odot$ seed at $z=30$
with an average Eddington ratio below 1.
\item For A2744-QSO1, replacing the adopted virial mass with the independent
dynamical estimate raises the required average Eddington ratio from 0.929 to
approximately 1. Its mass uncertainty allows average rates both below and
above the Eddington limit.
\end{itemize}

\section{Growth-model derivations and implementation}
\label{app:derivations}

\subsection{Accretion and exponential growth}

For electron scattering in ionized hydrogen, the Eddington luminosity,
Eddington ratio, and Eddington timescale are
\begin{equation}
L_{\rm Edd}=\frac{4\pi G\mbh m_p c}{\sigma_T},\qquad
\fedd=\frac{L_{\rm bol}}{L_{\rm Edd}},\qquad
t_{\rm Edd}=\frac{\mbh c^2}{L_{\rm Edd}}
=\frac{c\sigma_T}{4\pi Gm_p}\simeq0.45\ {\rm Gyr}.
\label{eq:eddington}
\end{equation}
Here $G$, $c$, $m_p$, and $\sigma_T$ denote the gravitational constant,
speed of light, proton mass, and Thomson cross section. With radiative
efficiency $0<\epsilon<1$ and rest-mass inflow rate $\dot M_{\rm in}$,
\begin{equation}
L_{\rm bol}=\epsilon\dot M_{\rm in}c^2,\qquad
\dot\mbh=(1-\epsilon)\dot M_{\rm in}
=\frac{1-\epsilon}{\epsilon}\frac{\fedd}{t_{\rm Edd}}\mbh.
\label{eq:massgrowth}
\end{equation}
The inflow rate is defined at the radiating flow; earlier mass loss is omitted.
Dividing Eq.~\ref{eq:massgrowth} by $\mbh$ and integrating at fixed
$\epsilon$ gives
\begin{equation}
\ln\!\left(\frac{\mbh}{B_{\rm merge}\mseed}\right)
=\frac{1-\epsilon}{\epsilon}\frac{1}{t_{\rm Edd}}
\int_{t(z_{\rm seed})}^{t(z_{\rm obs})}\fedd(t)\,dt
=\frac{1-\epsilon}{\epsilon}
\frac{\overline{f}_{\mathrm{Edd}}\,\Delta t}{t_{\rm Edd}}.
\end{equation}
Exponentiating gives Eq.~\ref{eq:growth}; solving for the average rate,
with a floor of zero, gives Eq.~\ref{eq:required-rate}.
For a constant positive $\fedd$, the e-folding time is
\begin{equation}
t_{\rm efold}=\frac{\epsilon}{1-\epsilon}\frac{t_{\rm Edd}}{\fedd}.
\end{equation}
It is 0.05 Gyr at $\epsilon=0.1$ and $\fedd=1$.

\subsection{Merger multiplier and inverse seed mass}

The implementation evaluates Eq.~\ref{eq:growth} in log mass:
\begin{equation}
\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{\mbh}{M_\odot}\right)
=\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{\mseed}{M_\odot}\right)
+\log_{10}B_{\rm merge}
+\frac{1-\epsilon}{\epsilon}
\frac{\overline{f}_{\mathrm{Edd}}\,\Delta t}{t_{\rm Edd}\ln 10}.
\label{eq:merger-implementation}
\end{equation}
We evaluate $B_{\rm merge}=1$ (no boost) and 2 (a factor-of-two mass boost).
At fixed accretion history,
\begin{equation}
\frac{\mbh(B_{\rm merge}=2)}{\mbh(B_{\rm merge}=1)}=2,
\qquad \Delta\log_{10}\mbh=\log_{10}2\simeq0.3010.
\label{eq:merger-shift}
\end{equation}

Solving the same relation for seed mass gives
\begin{equation}
\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{M_{\rm seed,req}}{M_\odot}\right)
=\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{\mbh}{M_\odot}\right)-\log_{10}B_{\rm merge}
-\frac{1-\epsilon}{\epsilon}
\frac{\overline{f}_{\mathrm{Edd}}\,\Delta t}{t_{\rm Edd}\ln 10}.
\label{eq:required-seed}
\end{equation}
Here $M_{\rm seed,req}$ is the seed mass required for the specified average
Eddington ratio, radiative efficiency, growth interval, and merger multiplier.

\subsection{Cosmic time}

Cosmic ages use the flat matter-plus-Lambda relation
\begin{equation}
t(z)=\frac{2}{3H_0\sqrt{\Omega_\Lambda}}
\operatorname{asinh}\!\left[\sqrt{\frac{\Omega_\Lambda}{\Omega_m}}
(1+z)^{-3/2}\right],
\label{eq:cosmictime}
\end{equation}
with $H_0=67.3\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}$, $\Omega_m=0.315$, and
$\Omega_\Lambda=0.685$. The Hubble constant is converted to inverse time;
ages and growth intervals are in Gyr. Radiation is neglected.

\subsection{Two-state duty cycle}

For a two-state history with the same efficiency in both states,
\begin{equation}
\overline{f}_{\mathrm{Edd}}=D f_{\rm burst}+(1-D)f_{\rm quiet},\qquad
D_{\rm req}=\frac{\overline{f}_{\mathrm{Edd,req}}-f_{\rm quiet}}
{f_{\rm burst}-f_{\rm quiet}}.
\label{eq:dutycycle}
\end{equation}
Here $D$ is the fraction of time spent active, with
$f_{\rm burst}>f_{\rm quiet}\geq0$. The expression applies when
$\overline{f}_{\mathrm{Edd,req}}\geq f_{\rm quiet}$. Values $D_{\rm req}>1$
identify histories that cannot reach the observed mass at the assumed rates.

\subsection{Mass uncertainties and growth-track plotting}
\label{app:implementation}

Sampling uses random seed 20260808 and a stable random stream for each object
identifier. With 10,000 draws, the Monte Carlo standard error of a probability
is at most 0.005, or about 0.0022 at a probability of 0.95; this reflects the
finite number of draws. The 12 NEXUS objects without reported mass errors
have repeated central values and zero-width stored intervals, but no
probabilities or uncertainty ranks. Table~\ref{tab:top} distinguishes separate
calibration scales from scatter already included in the quoted errors.

The mass-scale tests add the same fixed offset to every original mass draw.
The final published Killi mass
$8^{+0.5}_{-0.4}\times10^8M_\odot$ is converted by transforming both linear
bounds, giving $\log_{10}(\mbh/M_\odot)=8.90309^{+0.02633}_{-0.02228}$.

For Figure~\ref{fig:allgrowth}, we test average Eddington ratios from 0.1 to
2.0 in steps of 0.1. At each seed mass and fixed radiative efficiency, we plot
the 2 rates with the most primary objects within 0.5 dex of either merger
prediction ($B_{\rm merge}=1$ or 2), requiring at least 5 objects.
Ties favour smaller median absolute residuals, then smaller rates. Most
selected objects lie at $z\simeq4$--6. This selection is for display;
conclusions use the required-rate ranking. Reference rankings and
seed-mass--redshift maps use $\epsilon=0.1$.

\section{Compatibility across seed masses and accretion rates}
\label{app:compatibility}

\subsection{Efficiency prescription}
\label{app:efficiency}

The maps use the ideal Kerr thin-disc radiative efficiency at the innermost
stable circular orbit (ISCO; \citealt{bardeen1972}):
\begin{align}
\epsilon_a&=1-\sqrt{1-\frac{2}{3r_{\rm ISCO}}},\\
r_{\rm ISCO}&=3+Z_2-\operatorname{sgn}(a)
\sqrt{(3-Z_1)(3+Z_1+2Z_2)},\\
Z_1&=1+(1-a^2)^{1/3}\big[(1+a)^{1/3}+(1-a)^{1/3}\big],\qquad
Z_2=\sqrt{3a^2+Z_1^2}.
\label{eq:spin}
\end{align}
Here $a=cJ/(G\mbh^2)$ is the dimensionless spin, $J$ is the signed angular
momentum relative to the disc, and $r_{\rm ISCO}$ is measured in units of
$G\mbh/c^2$. Neglecting photon capture and stress at the ISCO gives
$\epsilon_a=0.03775$, 0.05719, and 0.42265 for $a=-1,0,+1$.
For supercritical inflow, we adopt a logarithmic luminosity relation motivated
by advection and wind models \citep{poutanen2007}, with coefficient 1 and no
radial mass loss:
\begin{equation}
\fedd=1+\ln\dot m,\qquad
\dot m=\frac{\dot M_{\rm in}}{L_{\rm Edd}/(\epsilon_a c^2)},\qquad
\epsilon_{\rm eff}=\begin{cases}
\epsilon_a,&\fedd\leq1,\\
\epsilon_a\fedd\exp(1-\fedd),&\fedd>1.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:effective-efficiency}
\end{equation}
The logarithmic relation applies only for $\fedd>1$. Each cell in
Figure~\ref{fig:compatibility} holds spin, Eddington ratio, and effective
radiative efficiency constant, so $\fedd=\overline{f}_{\mathrm{Edd}}$;
relativistic slim-disc dynamics are omitted. If efficiency varies, growth
depends on $\int [(1-\epsilon(t))/\epsilon(t)]\fedd(t)\,dt$ and requires
both histories.

\subsection{Seed-mass ranges and compatible fractions}
\label{sec:compatibility}

We solve Eq.~\ref{eq:required-seed} for each object's required seed mass and
check whether it lies within each of four intervals, including the endpoints:
$10$--$100\,M_\odot$ (light), $10^3$--$10^4\,M_\odot$ (intermediate),
$10^4$--$10^6\,M_\odot$ (heavy), and $10^2$--$10^6\,M_\odot$ (broad).
The grid uses $z_{\rm seed}=30$, spins $a=-1,0,+1$, merger multipliers
$B_{\rm merge}=1,2$, and average Eddington ratios 0.3, 1, and 2, with the
efficiency prescription in Section~\ref{app:efficiency}.

The intervals overlap and carry no probability weights; the broad interval
covers several possible seed-formation channels. A required seed below an
interval's lower bound is incompatible: even its smallest seed would grow
beyond the observed mass under those assumptions.
We report the percentage of objects whose required seeds fall within each
interval, using central mass estimates without propagating their errors.
These percentages describe the literature sample. Population fractions would
require the contributing studies' selection and detection probabilities.

The two samples show similar patterns (Fig.~\ref{fig:compatibility}). For
heavy seeds at $a=+1$ and $\fedd=1$, changing $B_{\rm merge}$ from 1 to 2
raises compatibility from 58 to 71 per cent in the primary sample and from
59 to 71 per cent in the exploratory sample. Higher accretion rates can
reduce compatibility when even the smallest seed grows beyond the observed mass.
